Things I've made

  • What's this?
    A ship's bridge you can actually drive. You take the wheel and the throttle and steer past real Great Lakes landmarks, with three screens as the windows. It's at the Outdoor Adventure Center in Detroit.
    What did I do?
    I scoped it and directed the build, from the sensors in the wheel and throttle to how the boat should feel on the water.
    Why three screens?
    The side screens angle in like a real bridge. nDisplay draws the right view for each one, so the horizon lines up instead of one picture getting stretched across all three.
    Kid-proof?
    A kid will slam the throttle. The sensor input is smoothed inside the engine, so even that reads as a real command.
    Made with
    Unreal Engine, nDisplay, custom wheel and throttle sensors. Installed 2026.

  • What's this?
    A 3,500 sq ft exhibit at the U.S. Space & Rocket Center about how forecasts actually get made. A 24' × 14' LED wall streams live weather data, the same feeds TV meteorologists use, and 54 screens of hands-on games fill the room.
    What did I do?
    I was on it from concept through install. I programmed the content and managed the content team.
    The tricky part?
    The wall runs my HTML layout through a video processor that crashes at the wrong resolution, so every block is pinned to its exact pixel area. If a feed drops, only that block goes quiet.
    Who sees it?
    About 800,000 visitors a year. It's been open since 2025.
    Made with
    Live NASA, NOAA and Baron data, HTML, a 24' × 14' LED wall, 54 screens.

  • What's this?
    Put a fish on the tray and the screen above switches to that species: walleye, bluegill, northern pike or smallmouth bass. It's at the Outdoor Adventure Center in Detroit.
    What did I do?
    I illustrated the fish, modeled them in 3D, and designed the screens they bring up.
    How does it know?
    Each fish has an RFID tag inside, and the reader picks it up. There's no button to explain. You put the fish down and the screen answers.
    Made with
    RFID, illustration, 3D modeling, screen design.

  • What's this?
    A wall about Toyota's people, places and cars across North America, at the Toyota experience center in San Antonio.
    What did I do?
    Six applications and six video channels that run as one piece, built inside Toyota's design system.
    The tricky part?
    Twelve things have to move together. The apps talk over WebSockets, a live connection that lets every screen react at the same moment. It was the most complex build in the center, and the goal was for nobody to notice.
    Made with
    Six synced apps, WebSockets, six video channels.

  • What's this?
