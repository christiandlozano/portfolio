Outdoor Adventure Center · Detroit, MI

Ship's Bridge Simulator

Take the helm. Three screens wrap the bridge as its windows; a physical wheel and throttle run through custom sensors into the latest distribution of Unreal Engine. Visitors pilot a vessel through open water — wind, wake, and momentum included — on a museum floor.

Field note

The hard part isn't the water shader — it's hardware that survives ten thousand hands. Sensor input is filtered and remapped in-engine, so a kid slamming the throttle reads as intent, not noise.

Unreal Engine · 3-screen bridge · sensor wheel + throttle · real-time water sim

U.S. Space & Rocket Center · Huntsville, AL

How We Know the Weather

A 24-foot LED wall with live weather radar, and 50+ screens of hands-on games — climate literacy for families at one of the country's busiest science centers.

Field note

I build the content, not the wall. My layout feeds a broadcast video processor that crashes on the wrong resolution, so every block is pinned to its exact pixel region — and a dropped feed fails quietly instead of taking the wall down with it.

Live weather data · 24-ft LED wall · 50+ screens · interactive games

Toyota Experience Center · Plano, TX

Toyota North America

Six synchronized applications and six video channels mapping a continent-scale brand story — the most complex build in the Center, designed to read as one simple story about people, places, and cars.

Field note

Six screens acting as one: every app stays in sync in real time, built end to end inside Toyota's design system. Complexity is the budget; simplicity is the product.

03 / About

Christian Lozano CH Experiences LLC

A studio of one. I design it, build it, and stand next to it when the doors open.

I make the software side of exhibits — web applications, games, interactive maps, video — for Toyota's experience centers, CALTY Design Research, the U.S. Space & Rocket Center, and Detroit's Outdoor Adventure Center.

Most of this work is judgment: what belongs on a screen, what belongs in the room, and what a visitor should feel in the few seconds they give you. I bring the taste to make those calls and the engineering to make them dependable long after opening night.

04 / How it ships

  • Value over novelty

    Technology isn't the question anymore — value is. An interactive earns its square footage or it doesn't ship.

  • Story first, systems underneath

    Place and narrative lead. Then the part nobody applauds: accessible to every visitor, working in more than one language, online or off, and still running when nobody's watching.

  • There on opening day

    One person accountable from the pitch to the punch list. If it breaks at 9 a.m. on a school-group Tuesday, that's my phone that rings.
